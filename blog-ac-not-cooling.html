Five reasons your AC runs but doesn't cool

An air conditioner can run while a room stays warm. The cause may be an incorrect setting, restricted airflow, heavy room load or a technical fault. These checks help you understand the symptoms without opening the equipment.

1. Confirm the mode and setpoint

Make sure the unit is set to Cool rather than Fan or Dry, and set the target below the current room temperature. Check that the remote display responds as expected.

2. Inspect the air filter

A dirty filter restricts airflow and can reduce cooling or contribute to coil icing. If the manual identifies it as user-serviceable, clean it as directed and allow it to dry fully before refitting.

3. Check for blocked airflow

Move curtains or furniture away from indoor vents. From a safe distance, check that debris is not obstructing the outdoor unit. Do not reach through the grille or remove electrical covers.

4. Notice ice, leaks or unusual sounds

Ice on coils or pipes, repeated water leakage, hissing or a sudden change in sound should be reported. If ice is visible, stop cooling and arrange a professional assessment rather than chipping it away or forcing the system to run.

5. Consider the room conditions

Open doors, direct sunlight, many occupants or a unit that is too small for the room can affect performance. Note whether the problem happens only during the hottest part of the day.

When to call a technician

If the basic checks do not help, a qualified technician can investigate refrigerant, coil, fan, compressor and electrical issues using the proper equipment. Do not attempt refrigerant charging or internal electrical repairs yourself.

Useful details: brand and model, room size, fault code, whether the outdoor unit runs, and when the problem started.
